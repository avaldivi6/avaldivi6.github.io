Teaching — summary & highlights

Teaching focus: curriculum development, large-enrollment instruction, and statistics education. I design courses to emphasize simulation-based inference, practical data analysis, and accessible materials for diverse learners.

Selected courses (highlights)

Course Notes
STAT-S 520 — Introduction to Statistics Large-enrollment course (399 students across many sections, 2017–2025). Redesigned for online asynchronous delivery (summer 2020) and added simulation-based inference (fall 2022).
STAT-S 631 — Applied Linear Models I Taught multiple sections (2014–2025). Major redesign and online offerings; emphasis on applied computation and reproducible analysis.
STAT-S 632 — Applied Linear Models II Follow-up advanced modeling course; includes projects and resampling methods.
STAT-S 100 — Statistical Literacy Introductory course for non-majors; focus on interpretation and communication of data.
STAT-S 301 — Statistics for Business Service course for business majors; emphasis on applied problem solving.
STAT-S 681 — Topics in Statistical Machine Learning Special topics graduate course; exposure to ensemble methods and modern supervised learning.

Pedagogical notes: curriculum development for STAT-S 520 and STAT-S 631 included asynchronous modules, problem-set redesign, and incorporation of simulation-first approaches. I maintain syllabi, sample assignments, and exam archives (available on request or to students via course site).

About

I am a Senior Lecturer in the Department of Statistics at Indiana University, Bloomington. My teaching centers on building accessible, simulation-forward courses for undergraduate and graduate students. I also maintain interests in statistical learning, generalized linear mixed models, and item response theory.

Office: Department of Statistics — Indiana University, Bloomington. Office hours: see course pages or contact me directly.
