About me

I am a senior lecturer in the Department of Statistics at Indiana University, where I teach undergraduate and graduate courses in statistics. My teaching interests focus on modern approaches to statistical inference, statistical learning, using statistics for experiential learning, applied linear models, and the classroom integration of computation and reproducible workflows.

I am a co-author of the textbook Statistical Inference via Data Science: A ModernDive into R and the Tidyverse (2nd Edition), which promotes a modern approach to teaching statistics and is used by instructors and programs beyond Indiana University.

In addition to teaching, I am actively involved in curricular development, program-level innovation, and professional development for educators at the national and international level.

Teaching

Overview

I am a faculty member in the Department of Statistics at Indiana University, where I teach undergraduate and graduate courses in statistics and data science. My interest focuses on modern approaches to statistical inference, applied linear models, and the integration of computation and reproducible workflows into statistics education. I am also the co-author of the textbook Statistical Inference via Data Science: A ModernDive into R and the Tidyverse (2nd Edition).

Across these courses, my approach emphasizes clear learning outcomes, alignment between instruction and assessment, and the integration of data-driven, reproducible workflows. I aim to help students develop both conceptual understanding and practical skills, preparing them to reason with data in academic, professional, and research settings. I am particularly interested in course and curriculum design, experiential learning opportunities, and evidence-based teaching practices that support student engagement and success at different stages of their academic careers.

Current Courses

STAT-S 631 & STAT-S 632 — Applied Linear Models I and II
A two-semester sequence for graduate students focused on studying, implementing, and interpreting linear models using inferential techniques, matrix-based methods, and R.

STAT-S 431 & STAT-S 432 — Applied Linear Models I and II
The undergraduate equivalent to STAT-S 631 & STAT-S 632.

STAT-S 350 — Applied Linear Models I
A self-contained, rigorous introduction to statistical inference aimed at undergraduate students who seek a deeper understanding of statistical reasoning and methodology.

STAT-S 519 An Introduction Introduction to Statistics in R
An online introductory statistics course for graduate students covering probability foundations and core inferential methods, including estimation and testing for 1- and 2-sample location problems, goodness-of-fit, correlation, and linear regression.

STAT-S 530 — Biometry
An applied second course in biometry that builds on prior statistical training to teach the design and analysis of biological studies, emphasizing appropriate modeling, implementation in R, interpretation of results, and clear scientific communication.

STAT-S 330 — Biometry
The undergraduate equivalent to STAT-S 530.
