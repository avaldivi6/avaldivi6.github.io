Teaching — summary & highlights

Teaching focus: curriculum development, large-enrollment instruction, and statistics education. I design courses to emphasize simulation-based inference, practical data analysis, and accessible materials for diverse learners.

Teaching Activities and Philosophy

My teaching spans undergraduate and graduate courses in statistics and data science, with an emphasis on applied modeling, statistical inference, and the use of modern computational tools. I regularly teach Applied Linear Models I and II (S431/S631 and S432/S632), introductory and intermediate courses in statistical inference (S350, S520), Biometry (S330/S530), and introductory statistics courses for data science graduate students, including online instruction in R (S519).

Across these courses, my approach emphasizes clear learning outcomes, alignment between instruction and assessment, and the integration of data-driven, reproducible workflows. I aim to help students develop both conceptual understanding and practical skills, preparing them to reason with data in academic, professional, and research settings. I am particularly interested in course and curriculum design, experiential learning opportunities, and evidence-based teaching practices that support student engagement and success at different stages of their academic careers.
